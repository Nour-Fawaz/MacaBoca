\subsection{Product Vision}

The purpose of this product is to give speech clinicians a single, centralized telehealth platform for reviewing, annotating, and analyzing speech assessments of bilingual children. 
By integrating SAFA’s annotation tools into the platform and incorporating patient records in one place, the product aims to make the assessment analysis process faster, simpler, and easier to use.

\subsubsection{Goals}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Integrate SAFA Into the MERLS platform: }Clinicians should be able to annotate assessments, meaning review and correct the written transcript of a recorded session, without leaving MERLS. SAFA is currently a standalone tool, and bringing it into the platform removes the need to switch between systems.
    \item \textbf{Improve SAFA’s Usability: }The annotation interface should be simple and guided enough that any speech clinician can use it with confidence, regardless of technical background.
    \item \textbf{Speed Up Speech Annotation: }Annotating a bilingual speech assessment should take significantly less time than manual transcription. SAFA has already been shown to reduce annotation time by roughly one-third, and the goal is to preserve or improve on that once SAFA is part of MERLS.
    \item \textbf{Provide a Clinician Record and Analysis Hub: }Clinicians should be able to find a patient’s records and past assessments in a centralized hub. This will simplify clinicians’ workflow, allowing them to quickly reference patient history without navigating multiple disconnected systems.
    \item \textbf{Protect Patient Data: }MERLS handles sensitive health information online; hence the system must keep patient data private and secure. Only authorized clinicians can access a patient’s records, recordings, and assessment results.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Existing platform:} The system must be built as an extension of the existing TeleHealth platform, including its React and Node.js web application and its Amazon S3 storage, rather than as a separate application. SAFA’s existing annotation features must be reused within this platform.

  \item \textbf{Development and test data:} Only synthetic data, or data that the research team has approved for development use, may be used during development and testing. Real patient recordings and records must not be stored in source code repositories, development environments, or test data.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{System Constraints}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Integration with MERLS:} The system must be integrated into the
  existing MERLS telehealth platform and accessed through the clinician dashboard,
  rather than as a separate standalone application. \textit{Rationale:} Clinician
  workflows require more than just the analysis of a single assessment. In order to 
  connect their work, they have should a platform that is able to help collect, review,
  transcribe, and annotate assessments across multiple patients and time.

\item \textbf{Web-based deployment:} The system must be a web application, deployed
  to a server and accessible through the web. The SAFA module, which is currently a
  Python desktop application, must be adapted to run within this web-based environment.
  \textit{Rationale:} MERLS is a web-based platform, so SAFA must also run in the
  browser to work within it. This also means clinicians can access the system from
  any device with a web browser, without needing to install or maintain a separate
  desktop application.

\item \textbf{Assessment transcriptions:} For automatic transcription within
  assessments, the system must use
  the pre-trained Whisper model under OpenAI, no other models will be trained and used for
  transcription. \textit{Rationale:} The research team's existing workflow uses 
  Whisper to generate the transcripts reviewed in SAFA, and training a new model 
  is outside the project scope.

\item \textbf{Health and privacy regulations:} The system must comply with all
  health and privacy regulations in the country that it's used in (e.g., HIPAA
  in the United States). \textit{Rationale:} The system stores
  recordings and records of children, which are protected health information.
  Leakage of such private information could have serious consequences for these
  families, as well as legal consequences for the research team.
\end{itemize}

\section{Stakeholder Requirements}

\noindent \begin{itemize}

\refstepcounter{srnum}
\item[\textbf{SR\thesrnum}] \label{SR_ReduceAnnotationTime} Clinicians want to spend less time transcribing and annotating recorded assessments, so they can focus on clinical analysis.

\refstepcounter{srnum}

\refstepcounter{srnum}
\item[\textbf{SR\thesrnum}] \label{SR_ClinicianFinalJudgment} Clinicians want a system that ``allow(s) the clinician to make the final judgement”. Automated transcripts, scores, and flags should act as suggestions that the clinician can review, accept, or correct.

\refstepcounter{srnum}
\item[\textbf{SR\thesrnum}] \label{SR_EvidenceTraceability} Clinicians want every result, flag, and transcript segment to be linked to the part of the recording it came from, so they have ``proof'' to support their clinical decisions.

\refstepcounter{srnum}
\item[\textbf{SR\thesrnum}] \label{SR_FlexibleWorkflows} Clinicians want to choose how in-depth they would like each assessment to be, whether that be full results first or partial transcription.

\refstepcounter{srnum}
\item[\textbf{SR\thesrnum}] \label{SR_CompiledReports} Clinicians want their scores, notes, transcripts, and selected evidence to be compiled into a clinical report for them, instead of assembling the report by hand from a spreadsheet.

\refstepcounter{srnum}
\item[\textbf{SR\thesrnum}] \label{SR_PatientPrivacy} Patients, guardians, and institutions want patient recordings and records to be accessible only to the clinicians responsible for them.

\refstepcounter{srnum}
\item[\textbf{SR\thesrnum}] \label{SR_LowLearningCurve} Clinicians want a system they can learn and use without formal training, regardless of their technical background.

\refstepcounter{srnum}
\item[\textbf{SR\thesrnum}] \label{SR_BilingualSupport} Clinicians want to analyze assessments that include speech that switches between two languages in order to compare a child’s performance in each language.

\refstepcounter{srnum}
\item[\textbf{SR\thesrnum}] \label{SR_ProgressOverTime} Clinicians want to see how a patient’s performance changes across assessments over time, as well as the results of a single assessment.

\end{itemize}

\section{User Personas}

\subsection{Overworked SLP in Favour of Automating the Speech Analysis Process}
\begin{itemize}
\item Name: James Caveat
\item Description: James is a speech clinician at a large hospital, where he is responsible for more than 30 patients. His heavy workload means he must review many speech assessments during his working hours, leaving him little time to carefully absorb and transcribe what he is watching. As a result, it is difficult for him to give each assessment the attention it deserves.
\item Goals: Speed up the analysis of speech assessments, receive assessment results quickly, obtain meaningful and clinically useful insights from each assessment, use a single system rather than learning and switching between multiple software tools, and have one simple place to organize his notes and patient information.
\item Frustrations: James is discouraged by the amount of time it takes to annotate speech data, exhausted by having to watch and listen to numerous assessment recordings from start to finish, struggles to organize assessment data he collects, and has trouble analyzing large amounts of unstructured data.
\item Technical Ability: James is comfortable with the basic tools he uses every day, but he is not technically fluent. He finds it difficult to move between different software systems and prefers to learn one tool well.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Detail-Oriented SLP Who Prioritizes Control Over Automated Analysis}
\begin{itemize}
\item Name: Mei Zhang
\item Description: Mei is a bilingual speech clinician with eight years of experience who runs a small private clinic. She and her staff deal with select clients that are more complex, where nearly all of them are bilingual children referred for a full diagnostic evaluation. Her main responsibility is deciding whether a child’s results indicate a language disorder. Her name is on every report that families, schools, and other professionals rely on. Due to the unique nature of any child's background, she does not accept a result at face value. She wants to see the evidence behind every score and flag, adjust the analysis to each child, and decide for herself how much of the process to automate. She values automation for removing repetitive work, but not for making decisions on her behalf.
\item Goals: Choose the level of automation for each assessment, understand and trace automated results to the exact moment in the recording, review and override outputs with a clear record of changes, add clinical interpretations for individual answers and the session as a whole, and build a comprehensive picture of each child’s strengths and needs to produce reports she can confidently defend.
\item Frustrations: Mei distrusts tools that present results without showing how they were reached, or that do not let her change them. She is frustrated by all-or-nothing automation, where she is forced to either accept an automated output entirely or redo the work from scratch. She worries that automated summaries can hide details that matter clinically, such as an unusual response or an ambiguous sound, and that corrections might overwrite the original output so she can no longer compare them. She is also frustrated when her notes and decisions are scattered across files and tools instead of being kept with the assessment they belong to.
\item Technical Ability: Mei is confident with technology and enjoys exploring a tool’s features in depth. She is willing to spend time learning a system if it gives her finer control over her work, and she expects to be able to customize how she reviews and organizes information. 
\end{itemize}

\refstepcounter{ucnum} \label{UC_ReviewTranscriptPlayback}
\subsection{\ucref{UC_ReviewTranscriptPlayback}: Review Transcript with Video Playback}

\textbf{Primary Actor}

Clinician, Researcher

\textbf{Preconditions}
\begin{itemize}
\item The user is logged into their account in the system
\item The user has opened a patient’s video assessment that has an auto-generated transcript
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Main Success Scenario}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The user opens the transcript of the assessment
\item The system displays the video playback alongside the transcript, divided into time-coded segments, and marks the start of each segment on the playback timeline
\item The user starts playback
\item The system highlights the segment being spoken at the current playback position and scrolls the transcript to keep it visible
\item The user notices a segment they want to review more closely and selects it
\item The system moves playback to the start of the selected segment and highlights it
\item The user replays the segment as many times as needed
\item The user pauses playback once they have finished reviewing
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Alternative Flows}
\begin{itemize}
\item[2a.] The transcript has not finished generating. The system displays the video playback and indicates that the transcript is still being generated. The use case resumes at step 2 once the transcript is ready.
\item[3a.] The user selects a segment before starting playback. The system moves playback to the start of that segment, and the use case continues at step 4 when the user starts playback.
\item[4a.] No speech occurs at the current playback position. The system does not highlight any segment until playback reaches the next segment.
\item[5a.] The user selects an assessment question instead of a segment. The system moves playback to the start of the portion of the recording in which that question was asked, and highlights the first segment in that portion.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Postconditions}
\begin{itemize}
\item The highlighted segment matches the current playback position.
\item The transcript and assessment results are unchanged.
\end{itemize}

\refstepcounter{ucnum} \label{UC_CorrectTranscriptChunk}
\subsection{\ucref{UC_CorrectTranscriptChunk}: Correct a Transcript Chunk}

\textbf{Primary Actor}

Clinician, Researcher

\textbf{Preconditions}
\begin{itemize}
\item The user is logged into their account in the system
\item The user is reviewing an assessment’s transcript alongside its video playback (\ucref{UC_ReviewTranscriptPlayback})
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Main Success Scenario}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The user selects a segment whose text does not match what is said in the recording
\item The system moves playback to the start of the segment, highlights it, and displays its details for editing: text, speaker, language, start time, and end time. The system indicates that the segment has not yet been verified.
\item The user replays the segment
\item The user corrects the text of the segment
\item The user changes the segment’s language tag to reflect the language spoken (English, Mandarin, or both)
\item The user saves the changes
\item The system saves the edited segment, keeps the original auto-generated version, and displays the differences between the two
\item The system marks the segment as verified and records who made the change and when
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Alternative Flows}
\begin{itemize}
\item[1a.] The user selects a segment that is already correct. The user marks it as verified without editing it, and the system marks it as verified and records who verified it and when.
\item[5a.] The segment’s start or end time is wrong. The user adjusts the times, and the system checks that the segment does not overlap the neighboring segments. If it does, the system shows a message and does not save the times until they are corrected.
\item[6a.] The user decides to discard their edits. The system restores the segment to its last saved version.
\item[6b.] The user tries to leave the transcript with unsaved changes. The system asks the user whether to save or discard them before leaving.
\item[8a.] The user later reverts a verified segment to its original auto-generated version. The system restores the original text, speaker, language, and times, and records the change.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Postconditions}
\begin{itemize}
\item The segment shows the user’s corrected version and is marked as verified.
\item The original auto-generated version of the segment is preserved and can be compared with the corrected version.
\item The system has recorded who changed the segment and when.
\end{itemize}

\refstepcounter{ucnum} \label{UC_ReturnAnnotationsToRecord}
\subsection{\ucref{UC_ReturnAnnotationsToRecord}: Return Annotated Results to the Patient Record}

\textbf{Primary Actor}

Clinician

\textbf{Preconditions}
\begin{itemize}
\item The clinician is logged into their account in the system
\item The clinician has reviewed and corrected the transcript of one of their patient’s assessments (\ucref{UC_CorrectTranscriptChunk})
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Main Success Scenario}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The clinician indicates that they have finished annotating the transcript
\item The system shows how many segments have been verified and how many have not
\item The clinician confirms that the annotation is complete
\item The system saves the annotated transcript to the patient’s assessment record and marks the transcript as verified
\item The system returns the clinician to the assessment’s results
\item The system shows the annotated transcript as part of the assessment, alongside its results and notes
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Alternative Flows}
\begin{itemize}
\item[2a.] Some segments have not been verified. The clinician chooses to return to the transcript, and the system moves to the first unverified segment. The use case resumes at step 1 once the clinician finishes.
\item[3a.] The clinician completes the annotation with unverified segments remaining. The system saves the transcript to the record and indicates which segments are still unverified.
\item[4a.] The clinician annotated only a selected portion of the recording (on-demand refinement). The system saves the annotated segments to the assessment record and returns the clinician to the assessment question they were reviewing.
\item[4b.] The system cannot save the annotation, for example because the network connection was lost. The system keeps the clinician’s work, informs the clinician that the save failed, and lets them try again.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Postconditions}
\begin{itemize}
\item The annotated transcript is stored in the patient’s assessment record without the clinician exporting, transferring, or importing any files.
\item The annotated transcript is shown when the assessment is opened from any of the clinician’s views, and is used when the clinician generates the assessment report.
\end{itemize}

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_Label6}:] The system shall allow clinicians to assign speaker roles and filter assessment transcripts by speaker.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Automatically generated transcripts do not identify who is speaking. Clinicians need to distinguish the child’s speech from that of adults, such as the parent or clinician, to analyze the child’s language and identify interfering parent behavior.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} The system must label every transcript segment from a speaker with the role assigned to that speaker once the clinician assigns it.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_Label7}:] The system shall generate detailed assessment reports from transcriptions, including any edits made by clinicians.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians review and correct the automatically generated transcription, adjust flagged assessment questions, and add notes to individual questions. The report must reflect these changes so that it includes the clinician’s judgment.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} The system must include any changes made throughout the analysis process made by the clinician, otherwise it outputs the automatically generated content in the report.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_Label8}:] The system shall allow clinicians to edit and update assessment results at any time.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians do not always analyze assessments sequentially. They may notice an error in a result or change their judgment after reviewing more of the recording, so they need to be able to go back and change results at any stage. 
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} The system must display the updated result once the clinician submits a change.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_Label9}:] The system shall display a list of all assigned patients to the logged-in clinician.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians care for multiple patients and need access to their information to properly care for each one. Additionally, they need a quick starting point for their work. 
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} The system must display every patient assigned to the clinician once they log in. The system must display an empty list when the clinician has no assigned patients.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_Label10}:] The system shall allow clinicians to add new patients.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians regularly take on new patients and need to create a profile for each one so that the patient’s assessments and records can be managed in the system.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} The system must add the new patient to the clinician’s list of patients once the clinician creates the profile.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_Label11}:] The system shall display to the clinician all completed assessments for a selected patient, ordered from most recent to oldest.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians must be able to track a patient's progress across multiple assessments over time.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} For a selected patient, all completed assessments are listed, ordered from most recent to oldest by assessment date.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_Label12}:] The system shall display an overview of every assessment's results, including automatically flagged items and the patient's overall results, without requiring a transcript.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} When reviewing many assessments, clinicians need a quick summary without having to perform any in-depth transcription and annotation.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} The overview for each completed assessment shows the patient's overall results, and each question's result (e.g., right or wrong) and whether it was flagged. The overview can be viewed before the assessment is reviewed in the SAFA tool.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_Label13}:] The system shall flag questions in video assessments where potential interference occurred.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} During assessment, outside help or disruptions (e.g., parents giving hints to the child) can heavily impact results and potentially make them invalid.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} Each question with detected interference is visibly marked in the assessment overview.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_Label14}:] The system shall allow the clinician to navigate to the point in the video assessment where a selected question was asked or a selected moment occurred.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} When searching for a specific moment or question asked in an assessment, when a transcript is available, clinicians should not have to search through a full recording to review that moment.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} Selecting a question or specific moment in a conversation starts video playback at that specific point in the assessment.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_Label15}:] The system shall allow the clinician to manually evaluate questions that cannot be automatically evaluated, using video playback of the recorded answer.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Some responses, such as open-ended answers, cannot be evaluated automatically and require clinical judgement. Other times, the system simply might not be able to automatically evaluate a question, so the clinician must manually evaluate it.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} For each question without an automatic result, the clinician can play the recorded answer and enter their evaluation, which is then saved to the assessment results.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_ManualEvaluation}:] The system shall allow clinicians to manually evaluate assessment questions that cannot be graded automatically, while playing back the portion of the recording in which the question was answered.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Some question types, such as repetition questions where the child repeats a sentence, have no single correct option that the system can compare against. The clinician must listen to and watch the child’s response to judge it, and the recording is the evidence for that judgment.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} For every question that is not automatically graded, the system must let the clinician play the recording segment for that question and record an evaluation (e.g., correct or incorrect). The saved evaluation must be shown with the question and included in the assessment’s score.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_QuestionNotes}:] The system shall allow clinicians to add, edit, and delete a note on each individual assessment question.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians record observations about specific answers, such as the type of error made or parent interference during that question. These observations support the final evaluation and must stay attached to the question they describe.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} After the clinician saves a note on a question, the system must show that note with the question every time the assessment is reopened, until the clinician edits or deletes it.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_AssessmentNotes}:] The system shall allow clinicians to add, edit, and delete notes on an entire video assessment.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Some observations apply to the whole session rather than a single question, such as the child’s attention, the home environment, or the recording quality. Clinicians need a place to record these alongside the assessment.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} After the clinician saves a note on an assessment, the system must show that note on the assessment’s view every time the assessment is reopened, until the clinician edits or deletes it.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_AnnotationsInRecord}:] The system shall store completed transcript annotations in the patient’s assessment record without the clinician manually exporting, transferring, or importing files.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} SAFA currently runs as a standalone tool, and its annotations exist only in files that clinicians export and move by hand. Annotations need to stay linked to the assessment and patient they belong to, so they can be reviewed later and used in reports.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} After a clinician saves an annotation, the annotation must appear in that patient’s assessment record when it is opened from any of the clinician’s views, with no file upload or download step performed by the clinician.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_TranscriptHighlight}:]The system shall highlight the transcript segment that corresponds to the current playback position of the assessment recording.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians verify transcripts by watching and listening to the recording. Highlighting the segment being spoken lets them follow along without searching for their place in the transcript.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} While the recording plays, the highlighted segment must be the segment whose start and end times contain the current playback position, and the transcript must scroll so that the highlighted segment is visible.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_SegmentSeek}:] The system shall move the recording’s playback position to the start of a transcript segment when the clinician selects that segment.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians often need to re-watch a specific moment, such as a child’s answer or a flagged interaction, to verify the transcript or their evaluation. Jumping directly to it avoids scrubbing through the recording by hand.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} After the clinician selects a segment, the playback position must be set to the segment’s start time, and that segment must be highlighted.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_Label22}:] The system shall display assessment results in a format suited to the assessment’s purpose.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians use progress-monitoring assessments to track change over time and diagnostic or screening assessments to evaluate a patient at a single point in time. Each purpose requires a different view of the results to be interpreted correctly.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} The system must show progress-monitoring results as change over time and diagnostic or screening results as a single-session summary.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_Label23}:] The system shall organize assessment results by clinically meaningful domains, such as semantics, grammar, and phonology.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians interpret a child's language abilities by domain, so grouping results this way supports diagnosis and reporting.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} Every question in an assessment is assigned to a domain, and the clinician can view results grouped and summarized by domain.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_Label24}:] The system shall ...
\\ \textbf{Rationale:}
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:}

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_Label25}:] The system shall provide breaks during assessments to keep children engaged.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Children with limited attention or complex communication needs may disengage during long screen-based assessments.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} The system presents an interactive break activity (e.g., an animation) after a set number of questions defined for each assessment, after which the assessment resumes where it left off.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_Label25}:] The system shall ...
\\ \textbf{Rationale:}
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:}

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_ResultVisualization}:] (Stretch goal) The system shall visualize a patient’s assessment results by clinical domain and language.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians who tested the platform valued charts and percentages, and asked for English and Mandarin results to be shown side by side and grouped into domains such as semantics, grammar, and phonology, to help tell a language difference from a disorder. This requirement is outside the current project scope and is linked to the Support Multiple Assessment Comparison stretch goal from the Problem Statement.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} For an assessment completed in both languages, the system must display the results for each clinical domain in English and Mandarin in a single view.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_LinguisticPatternDetection}:] (Stretch goal) The system shall automatically identify potential grammatical and phonological errors in the assessment transcript and present them to the clinician as suggestions for review.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians see transcription as an intermediate step, and they asked for the system to identify grammatical and error patterns so they can focus on interpretation. Since ambiguous speech (e.g., a dialect feature versus an articulation error) can be clinically meaningful, detections must remain suggestions that the clinician confirms. This requirement is outside the current project scope.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} Each detected error must be linked to its transcript segment and shown as unverified until the clinician accepts or rejects it. Only accepted errors may appear in the assessment report.

\subsection{Major Data Entities}

\plt{What information must exist for the system to accomplish its goals?}

\plt{For each entity, provide a short explanation.}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Clinician:} A user account for a speech-language pathologist. A clinician is assigned one or more patients.
\item \textbf{Patient:} A child being assessed. Each patient is assigned to one clinician and is linked to one guardian, and has zero or more assessments.
\item \textbf{Guardian:} The parent or caregiver account that the patient uses to complete assessments at home and that gives consent for recording.
\item \textbf{Question:} A test item in the question bank, defined by its language, assessment type (e.g., matching, repetition, quantifier), prompt media, and correct answer when one exists.
\item \textbf{Assessment:} A single completed test session by a patient. An assessment has one language and assessment type, one recording, one transcript, a set of question responses, and zero or more assessment notes.
\item \textbf{Question Response:} The patient’s answer to one question in an assessment, together with the clinician’s evaluation, interference flag status, and note for that question, and the portion of the recording in which the question was asked and answered. The transcript segments within that portion form the transcript for the question.
\item \textbf{Recording:} The single video and audio file captured during an assessment. Question responses, transcript segments, and interference flags all refer to positions on this recording’s timeline.
\item \textbf{Interference Flag:} A moment in a recording where the system detected possible interference (e.g., parent prompting), linked to the question during which it occurred.
\item \textbf{Transcript:} The written record of the speech in a recording. Each assessment has at most one transcript, made up of an ordered list of transcript segments.
\item \textbf{Transcript Segment:} A time-coded chunk of a transcript with its speaker and language, holding both the automatically generated version and the clinician’s edited version.
\item \textbf{Assessment Note:} A clinician’s note on an assessment as a whole.
\item \textbf{Report:} A clinical report generated from an assessment, compiling its scores, evaluations, notes, flags, and transcript at the time of generation.
\item \textbf{Audit Log Entry:} A record of a single view, change, download, or export of patient information by a user (\nfrref{NFR-S4}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Privacy and Data Retention}

The system stores recordings, transcripts, and health information about children, so the following apply to all data listed above:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Access control:} A clinician can only access the patients, assessments, recordings, transcripts, and reports of patients assigned to them (\nfrref{NFR-S1}). Guardians can only access their own child’s assessments on the patient-facing site.
  \item \textbf{Encryption:} Recordings, transcripts, and patient records are encrypted in transit and in storage (\nfrref{NFR-S3}). Recordings are accessed through links that expire after LINK\_EXPIRY minutes.
  \item \textbf{Consent:} A guardian must consent to the recording of assessments and their processing by third-party services before the patient can complete an assessment. The date of consent is stored with the guardian.
  \item \textbf{Third-party processing:} Audio is sent to external speech recognition and keyword detection services (Whisper and Deepgram). Voice recordings can identify a patient, so these services must be covered by a Business Associate Agreement (Section~\ref{sec_SystemConstraints}). Only the audio needed for transcription is sent, without names or other patient details.
  \item \textbf{Exports and reports:} Downloaded reports, transcripts, exported data, and video clips leave the system’s protection. Only the assigned clinician may export them, each export is recorded, and exported reports are marked as confidential (  \item \textbf{Researcher access:} \todo{Confirm with the supervisor whether researchers may see identifiable patient data (with guardian consent) or only de-identified data. This must be resolved before researcher access can be specified, since \nfrref{NFR-S1} currently limits all patient data to the assigned clinician.}
  \item \textbf{Development data:} Real patient data is never used in development or testing (Section~\ref{sec_SystemConstraints}).
  \item \textbf{Retention:} Patient records, recordings, transcripts, and reports are kept for RETENTION\_PERIOD years after the patient’s last assessment, after which they are deleted. Backups follow the same retention period (\nfrref{NFR-M1}).
  \item \textbf{Deletion:} When a guardian withdraws consent or requests deletion, the patient’s recordings, transcripts, and assessment data are deleted, including from backups, within DELETION\_PERIOD days, except where a regulation requires them to be kept.
  \item \textbf{Auditing:} Every view, change, download, and export of patient information is recorded with the user and time, and these records are kept for AUDIT\_LOG\_RETENTION years (\nfrref{NFR-S4}).
  \item \textbf{Compliance:} The system is designed to meet the HIPAA technical safeguards (\nfrref{NFR-CO1}). Administrative and physical safeguards, such as staff training and breach notification procedures, are the responsibility of the institutions that use the system and are outside the scope of this project.
\end{itemize}

\section{Non-Functional Requirements}

\subsection{Usability}

\noindent \begin{itemize}

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-U1}
  The system shall be intuitive for users with limited technical fluency.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians’ technical fluency varies, and they have little time to learn new software. An intuitive system shortens the learning curve and avoids the need for formal training.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} In usability testing, at least TASK\_SUCCESS\_RATE\% of clinicians with no prior training on the system must complete each core task (e.g., assign a speaker role, view assessment results, add a patient) without assistance within TASK\_TIME minutes.

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-U2}
  The system shall present all functionality with clear and descriptive labels.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians work under heavy workloads, and they may be unfamiliar with common software icons. Clear labeling lets them recognize what each function does without guessing.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} In usability testing, at least TASK\_SUCCESS\_RATE\% of clinicians must be able to locate and use each core function on their first attempt, relying only on the labels shown on screen and without assistance.

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-U3}
  The system shall allow clinicians to return to any previously visited stage of their workflow with minimal effort.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians do not analyze assessments in a straight line. They may go back to review or correct earlier work, so moving between stages should not slow them down.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} The clinician must be able to reach any previously visited stage of the workflow from the current page in at most MAX\_NAV\_STEPS navigation steps.

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-U4}
  The system shall immediately show clinicians the result of each change they submit.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians edit sensitive patient information; therefore, they need quick confirmation that the system received their change and that it was applied correctly. Without immediate feedback, they cannot tell whether their action was registered, which causes uncertainty and repeated attempts.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} The system must display the updated information to the clinician within SYSTEM\_FEEDBACK\_TIME seconds after the clinician submits a change.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Security}

\noindent \begin{itemize}

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-S1}
  The system shall only allow a clinician to view the information of patients assigned to them.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Patient assessments are sensitive health information. Access must be limited to the clinician responsible for the patient in order to protect patient privacy and meet data protection obligations.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} The system must only list patients assigned to the logged-in clinician and must not allow a clinician to add or access a patient who is in another clinician’s care.

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-S2}
  The system shall require users to re-enter their credentials after IDLE\_TIMEOUT minutes of inactivity.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians may leave their workstation unattended, and their accounts contain highly sensitive information. Automatic sign-out prevents unauthorized people from viewing it.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} The system must end the user’s session and require credentials before displaying any patient information once the user has been inactive for IDLE\_TIMEOUT minutes.

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-S3}
  The system shall encrypt patient recordings and transcripts in transit and in storage.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Assessment recordings and transcripts contain private patient information. The annotation service sends video assessments to a third-party transcription service, and the recordings are also kept in the system’s storage. Hence, they must be protected in both places so that anyone who intercepts the data or gains access to the storage cannot read it.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} The system must transmit all recordings and transcripts to third-party services using TRANSIT\_ENCRYPTION\_STANDARD and must store them using STORAGE\_ENCRYPTION\_STANDARD.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Maintainability}

\noindent \begin{itemize}

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-M1}
  The system shall regularly back up patient information and assessment recordings.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Assessment data is difficult or impossible to recreate, so losing it would waste clinicians’ work and could affect patient care.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} The system must back up all patient information and assessment recordings at least every BACKUP\_INTERVAL hours.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Recording Quality}

\noindent \begin{itemize}

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-P1}
  The system shall only accept video assessments in which the speakers can be clearly seen and heard.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} The annotation tool and behavior detection depend on the recording’s quality. Poor audio makes the auto-generated transcript less accurate, whereas poor video quality makes it difficult to detect faces and parent interference. Both instances can lead clinicians to wrong conclusions about a child’s assessment.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} The system must only accept submitted assessments where the recording at least meets the minimum video resolution, MIN\_VIDEO\_RESOLUTION, and the minimum audio level, MIN\_AUDIO\_LEVEL.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Accuracy}

\noindent \begin{itemize}

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-A1}
  The system shall raise automatic flags that are accurate enough for clinicians to use them to decide which parts of an assessment need review, with clinicians still making the final judgement.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} To make things easier, clinicians can use the flags in the assessment overview (\rref{FR_Label12}) or the interference markers (\rref{FR_Label13}) to quickly identify which segments need in-depth analysis. Too many missed flags mean certain signals go unnoticed by clinicians, while too many false flags waste clinicians' time.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} On a set of assessments reviewed by a clinician, the system flags at least FLAG\_DETECTION\_RATE\% of the moments the clinician identifies, and no more than FLAG\_FALSE\_ALARM\_RATE\% of its flags are false alarms. Each flag is placed within FLAG\_TIME\_TOLERANCE seconds of when the moment occurs in the recording.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Performance}

\noindent \begin{itemize}

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-PF1}
  The system shall respond quickly enough that clinicians can review assessments without noticeable waiting or lag.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians review many assessments in one sitting, so small delays can add up, especially if they are working on a tight schedule. Jumping to a question also needs to feel instant so clinicians can quickly compare responses.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} On a connection of at least MIN\_BANDWIDTH, the assessment overview (\rref{FR_Label12}) loads within OVERVIEW\_LOAD\_TIME seconds, and selecting a question (\rref{FR_Label14}) starts video playback at its timestamp within SEEK\_TIME seconds. During playback, the highlighted transcript chunk stays within SYNC\_TOLERANCE seconds of the video.
\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-P2}
  The system shall keep the transcript synchronized with the recording during playback and navigation.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians verify transcripts and evaluate answers by matching text to what they see and hear. If the highlighted segment lags behind the recording, or a selected segment starts at the wrong moment, clinicians may attribute speech to the wrong segment or question.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} For recordings up to MAX\_RECORDING\_LENGTH minutes long, the difference between the playback position and the start of the highlighted or selected segment must not exceed SYNC\_TOLERANCE milliseconds, and playback must begin within SEEK\_TIME seconds of the clinician selecting a segment.

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-UN1}
  The system shall make it clear to clinicians which content was generated automatically and which has been verified or edited by a clinician.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians emphasized that automated results are cues for review, not final judgments. To decide what still needs their attention and how much to rely on a result, they need to understand at a glance where each transcript segment, score, and flag came from.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} In usability testing, at least TASK\_SUCCESS\_RATE\% of clinicians must correctly identify which items on a screen are unverified automated content and which are clinician-verified, without assistance.

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-S4}
  The system shall record who accessed or changed patient information, and when.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} HIPAA requires a record of activity in systems that contain patient health information, so that unauthorized access can be detected and investigated. Assessment results also inform clinical decisions and reports, so clinicians may need to review how a result changed over time.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} For every view, change, download, or export of a patient’s records, recordings, transcripts, evaluations, notes, annotations, or reports, the system must store the identity of the user, the action performed, the item affected, and the date and time. These records must not be editable by clinicians or researchers and must be kept for AUDIT\_LOG\_RETENTION years.

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-CO1}
  The system shall meet the technical safeguards of the HIPAA Security Rule for the patient health information it stores and processes.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} The system stores recordings, transcripts, and assessment results of children, which are protected health information for U.S.-based clinicians. Removing names is not enough to de-identify this data: under HIPAA, full-face images and voice recordings can themselves identify a patient. Clinicians identified HIPAA compliance and control over who can access data as conditions for adopting the system, and institutions must approve tools before clinicians can use them \citep{CDC_HIPAA_1996}.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} Each HIPAA technical safeguard (access control, audit controls, integrity, person or entity authentication, and transmission security) must be mapped to at least one implemented mechanism in the system, and a review of the system against this mapping must find no unaddressed safeguard. Every third-party service that stores or processes patient health information, including recordings and audio, must be covered by a Business Associate Agreement (Section~\ref{sec_SystemConstraints}).

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-CA1}
  The system shall store the patients, assessments, recordings, and annotations of a full clinical caseload for every clinician using it.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians may each care for 30 or more patients, and every assessment adds a recording, a transcript, and annotations that must be kept for the full retention period. The system must hold this data without clinicians having to delete older assessments to make room for new ones.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} With MAX\_CLINICIANS clinicians, each with MAX\_CASELOAD patients who have completed ASSESSMENTS\_PER\_PATIENT assessments of MAX\_RECORDING\_LENGTH minutes each, the system must store all of the data and still meet \nfrref{NFR-U4} and \nfrref{NFR-P2}.

\end{itemize}

\section{AI and Machine Learning Requirements}

The system integrates a speech recognition model that was suggested and provided by the capstone team’s supervisors. 
The model was not created by the capstone team. \\

The model used is OpenAI’s Whisper-1, a general-purpose, multilingual automatic speech recognition and translation model. 
It is accessed through an API and takes audio or text as input \citep{OpenAI_Whisper}.

\subsection{Model Inputs}

Individual segments of the assessment’s audio file (.wav), each under 25 MB.

\subsection{Model Outputs}

A JSON object containing the start and end times of each segment, formatted as MM:SS (minutes:seconds), and a text field with the words spoken in the segment.

\subsubsection{Example Output}
\begin{verbatim}
  [
    {
    "start": "00:00",
    "end": "00:05",
    "text": "Recognized words."
    }
  ]
\end{verbatim}

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}

The model is not currently being evaluated. The annotation tool does, however, let speech clinicians edit transcriptions in chunks, viewing the text next to the audio playback, so incorrect auto-generated transcriptions can be corrected.

The model could be evaluated in the future by comparing the original auto-generated transcriptions against the clinician-edited versions and calculating the word error rate.


\subsection{Known Limitations}

The model’s website describes its performance as average, so the following failure modes are expected when it is used in MERLS \citep{Laumann_Whisper_2022}.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Incorrect transcription or translation:} since the model’s overall performance is average, some transcribed or translated text can be wrong. If these errors go unnoticed, clinicians may draw incorrect conclusions about a child’s speech abilities.
  \item \textbf{Incorrect transcription of difficult non-English words: }: the model is known to incorrectly transcribe difficult words in languages other than English. This is concerning for MERLS considering the system is used to assess bilingual children.
  \item \textbf{Uneven performance across different languages:} The model generally performs best on languages with a decent amount of training data, such as Spanish, German, Italian, and English. Transcript accuracy may vary by language, and languages with fewer resources may be transcribed less reliably.
\end{itemize}

Because of these expected failures, MERLS doesn’t treat the model’s output as final. 
Clinicians review the auto-generated transcript against the video playback and correct any errors before relying on it.

\subsection{Appendix A: Additional Materials}

This section presents key notes and quotes gathered during the requirements elicitation process for the project.


Several requirements were drawn from research papers describing the system and an experiment conducted by the project supervisor’s team in the United States. 
Although the papers have not yet been released, they describe the entire system and report usability tests with 10 speech clinicians. 
The following highlights the preferences and feedback participants shared while testing the MERLS system and the SAFA annotation tool.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Participant 2 expressed frustration with the amount of time it takes to transcribe assessments: “It would be perfect to have this feature, because doing the annotation part is very time consuming.”
  \item Participant 3 disliked transcribing speech assessments and was enthusiastic about saving time: “when you say that it decreases transcription time, I got very excited about having less time in analyzing speech samples.”
  \item Participant 3 also commented on how BeViz displays results: “I would definitely use the chart/graph/percentages.” This is in regard BeViz in terms of displaying results.
  \item Participant 3 further said they preferred having analysis results summarized as scores, summaries, and patterns rather than as a spreadsheet: “quick and helpful, instead of everything in a spreadsheet.”
  \item Participant 5 suggested what the report should contain: “The whole transcripts for all the [assessment] conversations would be useful to include in the report.”
  \item Participant 5 also raised a concern about data privacy: “the challenge will be the HIPAA, and who has access to data, and how the data could be shared with the parents and the educator.”
  \item Participant 7 appreciated the idea of auto-generated transcripts with human verification in the loop: “we can see a real-time transcription when we play the recording, so that we can do the analysis and give feedback.”
  \item Several clinicians emphasized keeping a human in the loop during the assessment analysis portion of the workflow. For example, Participant 9 stated, “[the system should] allow the clinician to make the final judgment; this is a tool that does not replace our judgment.”
  \item Several clinicians found the system straightforward. For example, participant 10 stated: “I think it’s straightforward. You can see the video recording, originals, [and] place to write notes.”
  \item Clinicians appreciated that the system covered the different aspects of the speech assessment workflow, particularly the ability to move from overall results to an individual question and review its prompt and response. Participant 10 stated, “you have the ‘proof’ there to support your clinical decision and your assessment.”
  \item Participants preferred to have results presented in both English and Mandarin side by side, and to have assessments organized meaningfully by semantics, grammar, and phonology. Participant 10 elaborated: “if you have a feature comparing two languages, side by side in the same graph that would probably be helpful to decide differences vs. disorder.”
\end{itemize}


\subsection{Appendix B: Development Plan}

Development of the system will be split into three phases, with each phase 
building on the previous one so that the most important goals are completed first.
Newer features are then added once the main system is complete and functional. 
The timing of each phase then follows the milestones outlined in the Development Plan.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Phase 1: Integration.} The first phase focuses on integrating SAFA
  into the existing MERLS platform, so clinicians can review and annotate assessments
  without ever having to leave the platform. This includes connecting SAFA's existing transcription
  and annotation Python modules to MERLS's patient records and recorded assessments,
  and making sure that patient data stays secure as it moves in between. This phase
  covers the \textit{Integrate SAFA Into the MERLS platform} and \textit{Protect
  Patient Data} goals, and will be demonstrated in the proof of concept.

  \item \textbf{Phase 2: Enhance the SAFA User Interface.} Once SAFA is
  integrated, the second phase focuses on improving its interface so that
  speech clinicians can use it with ease, regardless of their knowledge and
  technical expertise. This involves making the annotation workflow simpler and more
  intuitive, and requires addressing feedback from clinicians on the current SAFA tool (see
  Appendix A). This phase covers the \textit{Improve SAFA's Usability} and
  \textit{Speed Up Speech Annotation} goals.

  \item \textbf{Phase 3: New Features.} The team will add new features that extend the system beyond
  SAFA's current capabilities, such as the stretch goals from the Problem
  Statement. This includes generating patient-facing assessment reports and comparing multiple
  assessments for the same patient over time. Any features not completed during
  the course will be documented as future work.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Nour}
\subsubsection*{Question 1}
Writing the functional requirements was more straightforward than we expected because we had enough resources to understand what our supervisors wanted from the project. 
They provided us with a couple of unreleased research papers that described the system architecture, clinician workflows, including a study they conducted on 10 clinicians to test the TeLaC (now MERLS) platform. 
With this information we were able to create a mental model of the MERLS, SAFA, and BeViz modules and what the purpose for each is. 
Moreover, our supervisors provided us with the GitHub repositories of the annotation tool SAFA and the MERLS system (which is the overall TeleHealth system). 
That let us write requirements that were realistic in how the system behaves and how it should behave once we integrate SAFA. 
Additionally, it made it easier to write use cases that reflected the different clinician workflows detailed in the research papers which are, direct analysis, annotation first analysis, and on-demand refinement. 
These three are the main important workflows and should be enabled by the system very seamlessly. 
Moreover, after speaking with our supervisors, we also learned which areas they are prioritizing and what the main features of the updated system should be. 
This helped us determine what requirements could change and what shouldn’t change in the system. 

\subsubsection*{Question 2}
Creating user personas was my biggest challenge. 
The part of the system our group is working on is mainly for clinicians, so it was hard to identify different types of users, and we were worried the personas would end up looking nearly identical. 
Hence, we created different “types” of clinicians who might use the platform and differentiated them by their work environment and capabilities, such as how heavy their workload is and how comfortable they are with technology. 
This gave us personas that reflect real differences in how the system might be used, which also helped us write non-functional requirements around usability.

Additionally, deciding how detailed each use case should be was also a bit difficult. 
I wasn’t sure where in the workflow each one should start, and simulating the whole workflow from when the clinician enters the system felt like too much. 
It would have repeated the same steps, such as logging in and selecting a patient in every use case. 
Hence, I scoped each use case to a single clinician goal and used the preconditions to set the starting point. 
For example, the clinician is already logged in and has opened a patient’s assessment. 
I also kept the steps focused on what the clinician does and how the system responds, and left out screen-level details, since the interface may change during the redesign.

\subsubsection*{Question 4}
I took SFWRENG 4AL3, the applications of machine learning course. 
It helped me understand the kinds of models our supervisors may have used when building the SAFA tool, and I am comfortable with the machine learning terminology in our documents, such as word error rate and model inputs and outputs. 
Although we did not develop the model and it is not currently being evaluated, I understand how the audio files are segmented and sent to the model for transcription, and I can reason about its known limitations, such as weaker performance on non-English words. 
This is relevant because our users work with bilingual children. The course also taught me why disclosing AI use matters. 
We learned about the negative effects of large language models, including their large carbon footprint, and about the importance of being transparent about how they were used in our documents. 

\subsubsection*{Question 5}
Team management is extremely important for completing the project successfully. 
Our work is split across documents for different milestones, and each member’s section has to fit with the others, so we need a clear picture of who is doing what and when it is due. 
Having good team management lets us stay on top of tasks, stay on the same page, and catch problems early on. 
Moreover, it means regular meetings where each member explains what they have completed since the last one, and discussion regarding the next project steps. 
This is important considering we are balancing the project along with our other courses. 
Furthermore, considering we are building on an existing system with already busy supervisors, we need to communicate regularly with them and record their feedback during meetings. 
Without this, it would be easy for sections to become inconsistent with each other and for team members to be lost or have a different understanding of the project vision and next steps.

Additionally, understanding of how SAFA uses AI to create transcriptions is also important. 
Integrating SAFA into the existing MERLS system is the main focus of our project, so we need to understand how the audio is sent to the model, what the model returns, and where its transcriptions may be wrong, so we can design the system around this.

It keeps the team on track and helps us balance the project with other courses. 
Another is writing meeting minutes after each meeting.
