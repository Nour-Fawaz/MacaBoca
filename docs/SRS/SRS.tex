\section{Introduction} 
% ==============================================================

This document specifies the software requirements for \progname{}, a platform for remote speech and language assessment of bilingual children.
This document follows the course IEEE SRS template, which is based on the IEEE recommended practice for software requirements specifications (SRS), incorporating the overall life-cycle context
of current requirements engineering practice.

\subsection{Purpose}

The purpose of \progname{} is to solve the usability and workflow challenges faced by speech-language pathologists (SLPs) conducting remote speech assessments of bilingual pediatric patients. The lack of integration between assessment recordings and annotation tools, combined with limitations in the usability of existing tools, can make the assessment process inefficient and difficult to manage, potentially affecting the accuracy and consistency of clinical annotations.

\subsection{Scope}

Currently, assessment recordings on the TeLaC platform and the existing software tool (SAFA) used by clinicians to annotate and analyze these recordings are not fully integrated. Additionally, the existing annotation tool has usability limitations that can make the assessment process less intuitive and efficient.

The intended users are speech-language pathologists conducting 
remote speech assessments of bilingual pediatric patients through 
telehealth. Speech and language researchers are secondary users who review the assessment data. The software will integrate speech assessment annotation 
functionality into the existing TeLaC platform, providing clinicians 
with a centralized interface to review assessment recordings, edit automatically generated transcriptions, annotate speech, and export annotation data.

The software aims to streamline the clinical workflow by reducing the need to switch between separate tools, improving the usability of annotation features, and supporting the organization and review of assessment results. These improvements are intended to help clinicians conduct assessments more efficiently and consistently while supporting the ongoing monitoring of pediatric patients' speech development.

The scope of \progname{} is intended to accommodate all SLPs who use the TeLaC platform for the supported assessment workflow, regardless of their individual clinical needs or the number of patients they assess. It is 
limited to Mandarin-English bilingual speech assessments; other language combinations are not supported. Assessments involving other language combinations are not expected to be supported. The software is intended to assist clinicians with transcription and annotation rather than independently diagnose speech disorders or replace professional clinical judgment.
Lastly, training new speech recognition models is outside the scope of this project.                                    

\subsection{Stakeholders}

The following stakeholders have an interest in the integrated TeLaC system.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Customer and Project Sponsor:} Dr.\ Irene Yuan and the research team (Dr.\ Du, Dr.\ Wu, and collaborators) who designed the TeLaC platform, SAFA, and BeViz.
They require an integrated system that supports multiple clinician workflows for analyzing uploaded assessments, is consistent with their existing data collection pipeline, and can be extended in future research.

\item \textbf{End Users (Speech-Language Pathologists):} The primary users of the system.
SLPs review the recorded assessments, evaluate answers, verify and correct auto-generated transcripts, add notes, and use the results to make diagnostic decisions by writing clinical reports.

\item \textbf{Secondary Users (Researchers):} Speech and language researchers who study assessment data and evaluate how well the technology supports clinicians.
They need access to assessments at every stage of analysis in order to determine the accuracy and efficacy of a software.

\item \textbf{Patients and Guardians:} Bilingual children who complete assessments at home on the patient-facing site, supervised by a parent or guardian.
They do not use the clinician side of the system, but they depend on it to keep their recordings private and to be assessed accurately and promptly.

\item \textbf{Development Team:} Team 24 (Maca Boca), who design, implement, test, and maintain the system during the project.
They need clear, testable, and traceable requirements, as well as access to the existing TeleHealth and SAFA code bases.

\item \textbf{Institutions and Regulatory Bodies:} Clinics, schools, and hospitals that employ SLPs, and regulators of health information (e.g., HIPAA \citep{CDC_HIPAA_1996} for U.S.-based clinicians).
They must approve tools that handle patient data and need the system to meet privacy, consent, and data retention obligations.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Problem Statement}

\plt{Current situation, problem, impact, desired improvement.  You will likely borrow some text from your problem description document.}

Currently, speech-language pathologists (SLPs) conducting remote assessments of bilingual pediatric patients face challenges annotating, managing, and analyzing patient assessments through telehealth. These challenges can limit assessment efficiency and consistency, particularly in pediatric telehealth settings where clinicians rely on digital tools to evaluate and monitor speech development. The existing annotation tool, SAFA, is hosted locally and operates separately from the TeLaC telehealth platform used by patients and their guardians. This separation creates a disjointed workflow between patient assessments and clinician annotation. Additionally, SAFA has a limited and unintuitive user interface, resulting in a poor user experience for clinicians. Its functionality also does not fully support the clinician-side assessment, record-keeping, and analysis workflows required by SLPs.

Overall, the lack of integration between patient assessments and clinician tools, combined with limitations in interface usability and functionality, creates barriers to an efficient and cohesive speech assessment workflow.

\subsection{Product Vision}

The purpose of this product is to give speech clinicians a single, centralized telehealth platform for reviewing, annotating, and analyzing speech assessments of bilingual children. 
By integrating SAFA’s annotation tools into the platform and incorporating patient records in one place, the product aims to make the assessment analysis process faster, simpler, and easier to use.

\subsubsection{Goals} \label{sec:Goals}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Integrate SAFA Into the TeLaC platform: }Clinicians should be able to annotate assessments, meaning review and correct the written transcript of a recorded session, without leaving TeLaC. SAFA is currently a standalone tool, and bringing it into the platform removes the need to switch between systems.
    \item \textbf{Improve SAFA’s Usability: }The annotation interface should be simple and guided enough that any speech clinician can use it with confidence, regardless of technical background.
    \item \textbf{Speed Up Speech Annotation: }Annotating a bilingual speech assessment should take significantly less time than manual transcription. SAFA has already been shown to reduce annotation time by roughly one-third, and the goal is to preserve or improve on that once SAFA is part of TeLaC.
    \item \textbf{Provide a Clinician Record and Analysis Hub: }Clinicians should be able to find a patient’s records and past assessments in a centralized hub. This will simplify clinicians’ workflow, allowing them to quickly reference patient history without navigating multiple disconnected systems.
    \item \textbf{Protect Patient Data: }TeLaC handles sensitive health information online; hence the system must keep patient data private and secure. Only authorized clinicians can access a patient’s records, recordings, and assessment results.
\end{enumerate}


  
\subsection{System Context}

Figure~\ref{Fig_SystemContext} shows the context between the Speech Assessment
Web Platform (TeLaC) and the users and external systems it interacts with. The platform
integrates SAFA into TeLaC, which was previously known as TeleHealth. Three types
of users will interact with the platform directly, the first being patients and caregivers; they'll
complete assessments by submitting video and audio responses, and will receive
assessment reports as they progress. Clinicians review transcripts 
and flagged insights from the system, then edit and annotate them to build reports for 
their patients. Researchers will correct transcripts to gain insights on how flagging,
transcription, and other features can be improved. They also gain data for their research
on speech and language patterns.
The platform also relies on speech and language models, which generate draft transcripts
and detect meaningful insights and patterns within a recording. The database and 
file storage service is also used to securely store the data that the platform relies on.

The responsibilities of each entity in the system context are as follows:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Patient and Caregiver Responsibilities:}
\begin{itemize}
\item Complete assessments by submitting video and audio responses.
\item Record in a setting where the child can be clearly seen and heard.
\end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Clinician Responsibilities:}
\begin{itemize}
\item Review flagged items, transcripts, and annotations before relying on them.
\item Provide manual evaluations, edits, and notes on assessment results.
\item Make the final clinical judgement for each patient.
\end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Researcher Responsibilities:}
\begin{itemize}
\item Provide transcript corrections and speaker and language labels.
\item Handle exported data according to research ethics requirements.
\end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Speech and Language Model Responsibilities:}
\begin{itemize}
\item Return draft transcripts and detected patterns for a given recording.
\end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Database and File Storage Responsibilities:}
\begin{itemize}
\item Securely store and return recordings and patient records.
\end{itemize}
\item \textbf{System Responsibilities:}
\begin{itemize}
\item Check that submitted recordings meet minimum quality requirements.
\item Restrict access to patient data based on the user's role.
\item Send recordings to the speech and language models and display the results.
\item Generate assessment overviews, reports, and data exports.
\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\progname{} will be used in a clinical context, where clinicians review and annotate
remote assessments as a part of working with their patients. It will also bes used in 
scientific work by researchers studying bilingual speech therapy and the effectiveness 
of software-assisted annotation for remote assessments.
Use of the system in a clinical setting means that errors may have serious consequences.
An inaccurate transcription or flagged item may lead to wrong diagnosis. The failure to 
securely store and return patient data may lead to exposing recordings and health records of children. Thus, while
the system is not safety-critical, as it does not make diagnoses and cause direct physical harm to patients, accuracy
and clinician control over the system is important.


\subsection{User Characteristics} \label{SecUserCharacteristics}

The system has two main types of users. The usability requirements (e.g., \nfrref{NFR-U1}) are measured against the typical users described below.

\subsubsection{Speech-Language Pathologists}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Education:} A graduate degree in speech-language pathology and a licence or certification to practice.
  \item \textbf{Experience:} 1 to 10 years of clinical practice with children aged 0 to 18, in schools, hospitals, or private clinics. This is based on the clinicians in the research team’s study (Appendix~A).
  \item \textbf{Domain knowledge:} Regularly conducts child language assessments 
  (e.g., the Preschool Language Scales) and understands language sample analysis terms such as utterance, morpheme, and code-switching.
  \item \textbf{Language:} Fluent in English. Clinicians who verify another language must also have complete fluency in that language.
  \item \textbf{Technical ability:} Uses everyday office and web software on a desktop or laptop computer. No programming experience or knowledge of speech recognition is expected.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Researchers}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Education:} A graduate degree, or graduate study, in speech-language pathology, linguistics, or a related field. 
  \item \textbf{Technical ability:} Is familiar with spreadsheets, editing tools, and exported data files such as CSV. No programming experience is expected.
  \item \textbf{Role:} Reviews and aggregates assessment data, and does not make clinical decisions.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{System Constraints} \label{sec:SystemConstraints}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Integration with TeLaC:} The system must be integrated into the
  existing TeLaC telehealth platform and accessed through the clinician dashboard,
  rather than as a separate standalone application. \textit{Rationale:} Clinician
  workflows require more than just the analysis of a single assessment. In order to 
  connect their work, they have should a platform that is able to help collect, review,
  transcribe, and annotate assessments across multiple patients and time.

\item \textbf{Web-based deployment:} The system must be a web application, deployed
  to a server and accessible through the web. The SAFA module, which is currently a
  Python desktop application, must be adapted to run within this web-based environment.
  \textit{Rationale:} TeLaC is a web-based platform, so SAFA must also run in the
  browser to work within it. This also means clinicians can access the system from
  any device with a web browser, without needing to install or maintain a separate
  desktop application.

\item \textbf{Assessment transcriptions:} For automatic transcription within
  assessments, the system must use
  the pre-trained Whisper model under OpenAI, no other models will be trained and used for
  transcription. \textit{Rationale:} The research team's existing workflow uses 
  Whisper to generate the transcripts reviewed in SAFA, and training a new model 
  is outside the project scope.

\item \textbf{Health and privacy regulations:} The system must comply with all
  health and privacy regulations in the country that it's used in (e.g., HIPAA
  in the United States). \textit{Rationale:} The system stores
  recordings and records of children, which are protected health information.
  Leakage of such private information could have serious consequences for these
  families, as well as legal consequences for the research team.
\end{itemize}

\section{Stakeholder Requirements}
This section provides requirements directly stated by our stakeholders in documented interviews, as referenced in Appendix A.

\noindent \begin{itemize}

\refstepcounter{srnum}
\item[\textbf{SR\thesrnum}] \label{SR_ReduceAnnotationTime} Clinicians want to spend less time transcribing and annotating recorded assessments, so they can focus on clinical analysis.
Clinicians reported that annotating a 20-minute session can take up to four hours, and that the annotation part is ``very time consuming''.

\refstepcounter{srnum}
\item[\textbf{SR\thesrnum}] \label{SR_SinglePlatform} Clinicians want to review, annotate, analyze, and report on an assessment in one place, without having to switch between tools or moving files between them.

\refstepcounter{srnum}
\item[\textbf{SR\thesrnum}] \label{SR_ClinicianFinalJudgment} Clinicians want a system that ``allow(s) the clinician to make the final judgement”. Automated transcripts, scores, and flags should act as suggestions that the clinician can review, accept, or correct.

\refstepcounter{srnum}
\item[\textbf{SR\thesrnum}] \label{SR_EvidenceTraceability} Clinicians want every result, flag, and transcript segment to be linked to the part of the recording it came from, so they have ``proof'' to support their clinical decisions.

\refstepcounter{srnum}
\item[\textbf{SR\thesrnum}] \label{SR_FlexibleWorkflows} Clinicians want to choose how in-depth they would like each assessment to be, whether that be full results first or partial transcription.

\refstepcounter{srnum}
\item[\textbf{SR\thesrnum}] \label{SR_CompiledReports} Clinicians want their scores, notes, transcripts, and selected evidence to be compiled into a clinical report for them, instead of assembling the report by hand from a spreadsheet.

\refstepcounter{srnum}
\item[\textbf{SR\thesrnum}] \label{SR_PatientPrivacy} Patients, guardians, and institutions want patient recordings and records to be accessible only to the clinicians responsible for them.

\refstepcounter{srnum}
\item[\textbf{SR\thesrnum}] \label{SR_LowLearningCurve} Clinicians want a system they can learn and use without formal training, regardless of their technical background.

\refstepcounter{srnum}
\item[\textbf{SR\thesrnum}] \label{SR_BilingualSupport} Clinicians want to analyze assessments that include speech that switches between two languages in order to compare a child’s performance in each language.

\refstepcounter{srnum}
\item[\textbf{SR\thesrnum}] \label{SR_ProgressOverTime} Clinicians want to see how a patient’s performance changes across assessments over time, as well as the results of a single assessment.

\end{itemize}

\section{User Personas} \label{sec:UserPersonas}

\subsection{Overworked SLP in Favour of Automating the Speech Analysis Process}
\begin{itemize}
\item Name: James Caveat
\item Description: James is a speech clinician at a large hospital, where he is responsible for more than 30 patients. His heavy workload means he must review many speech assessments during his working hours, leaving him little time to carefully absorb and transcribe what he is watching. As a result, it is difficult for him to give each assessment the attention it deserves.
\item Goals: Speed up the analysis of speech assessments, receive assessment results quickly, obtain meaningful and clinically useful insights from each assessment, use a single system rather than learning and switching between multiple software tools, and have one simple place to organize his notes and patient information.
\item Frustrations: James is discouraged by the amount of time it takes to annotate speech data, exhausted by having to watch and listen to numerous assessment recordings from start to finish, struggles to organize assessment data he collects, and has trouble analyzing large amounts of unstructured data.
\item Technical Ability: James is comfortable with the basic tools he uses every day, but he is not technically fluent. He finds it difficult to move between different software systems and prefers to learn one tool well.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Detail-Oriented SLP Who Prioritizes Control Over Automated Analysis}
\begin{itemize}
\item Name: Mei Zhang
\item Description: Mei is a bilingual speech clinician with eight years of experience who runs a small private clinic. She and her staff deal with select clients that are more complex, where nearly all of them are bilingual children referred for a full diagnostic evaluation. Her main responsibility is deciding whether a child’s results indicate a language disorder. Her name is on every report that families, schools, and other professionals rely on. Due to the unique nature of any child's background, she does not accept a result at face value. She wants to see the evidence behind every score and flag, adjust the analysis to each child, and decide for herself how much of the process to automate. She values automation for removing repetitive work, but not for making decisions on her behalf.
\item Goals: Choose the level of automation for each assessment, understand and trace automated results to the exact moment in the recording, review and override outputs with a clear record of changes, add clinical interpretations for individual answers and the session as a whole, and build a comprehensive picture of each child’s strengths and needs to produce reports she can confidently defend.
\item Frustrations: Mei distrusts tools that present results without showing how they were reached, or that do not let her change them. She is frustrated by all-or-nothing automation, where she is forced to either accept an automated output entirely or redo the work from scratch. She worries that automated summaries can hide details that matter clinically, such as an unusual response or an ambiguous sound, and that corrections might overwrite the original output so she can no longer compare them. She is also frustrated when her notes and decisions are scattered across files and tools instead of being kept with the assessment they belong to.
\item Technical Ability: Mei is confident with technology and enjoys exploring a tool’s features in depth. She is willing to spend time learning a system if it gives her finer control over her work, and she expects to be able to customize how she reviews and organizes information. 
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Curious Researcher Interested in Software Assisting Speech Therapy}
\begin{itemize}
\item Name: Georgia Curios
\item Description: Georgia is a speech therapy researcher that has worked in the field for over three decades. Throughout her career, she has witnessed the benefits and limitations of technological advancements in speech therapy. Unlike clinicians, who primarily focus on conducting individual patient assessments, Georgia is interested in evaluating the broader effectiveness, usability, and performance of speech therapy software. She seeks to understand how technology supports clinical practice, maintains assessment reliability, and contributes to patient outcomes over time.
\item Frustrations: Georgia is overwhelmed by the numerous software tools available, as they are hosted on separate platforms. She can only view one assessment at a time, making it difficult to compare multiple assessments. She also finds it difficult to identify trends in patient outcomes using current data visualization and aggregation tools. Additionally, she is uncertain about the accuracy and reliability of software-assisted annotation.
\item Technical Ability: Georgia is technically proficient and experienced with speech therapy software. Although she is adaptable to new technologies, she compares their capabilities and features with existing tools and traditional practices. She is also comfortable interpreting assessment data and performance metrics, but cannot fully verify clinical decisions.
\end{itemize}

\section{Use Cases} \label{sec:UseCases}
\plt{Use cases are a way to describe the interactions between users and the system.

\refstepcounter{ucnum} \label{UC_Label1}
\subsection{\ucref{UC_Label1}: Log In}

\textbf{Primary Actor}

Clinician, Researcher

\textbf{Preconditions}
\begin{itemize}

\section{Functional Requirements} \label{sec:FunctionalRequirements}

\noindent \begin{itemize}

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_AutomaticTranscription}:] The system shall automatically generate a text transcription for uploaded patient assessment videos.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Manually transcribing assessment recordings is time-consuming and increases the workload of speech-language pathologists (SLPs). Since transcription provides the foundation for subsequent annotation and analysis, automating this task allows clinicians to focus on reviewing and interpreting speech rather than manually transcribing recordings.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} For every successfully uploaded supported assessment video, the system shall automatically generate a corresponding text transcription without requiring clinician intervention and make the transcription available for clinician review upon completion.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_TranscriptionEditing}:] The system shall allow clinicians to edit automatically generated transcriptions.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Automatically generated transcriptions may contain errors or inaccuracies, particularly with code-mixing bilingual patients. Clinicians require the ability to correct transcription errors to ensure that subsequent annotations and analyses are based on accurate text.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} Clinicians shall be able to add, delete, and modify transcription text and save their changes. Saved changes shall persist when the recording is reopened for assessment.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_AnnotationExport}:] The system shall allow clinicians to export annotation data from assessments.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians need to record, review, and share assessment results for documentation, patient monitoring, and further analysis. Exporting annotation data reduces reliance on manually transferring information between tools and supports record-keeping.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} Clinicians shall be able to export annotation data from an assessment in a supported file format. The exported file shall contain all approved annotations associated with that assessment.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_SpeakerIdentification}:] The system shall distinguish between different speakers in video transcriptions.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Remote speech recordings may involve multiple speakers, including the child and their guardian. Distinguishing between speakers helps SLPs annotate the correct individuals and account for distractions or outside influences.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} For transcriptions containing multiple identified speakers, the system shall visually distinguish each speaker using consistent labels, tags, or highlighting. Clinicians shall be able to determine which identified speaker corresponds to each displayed utterance.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_AssessmentWorkflowNavigation}:] The system shall allow clinicians to navigate between different stages of the speech assessment analysis workflow.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} The assessment process involves multiple stages, from reviewing and editing recordings to exporting results. Clear navigation helps clinicians move between these stages, understand their current progress, and complete assessments more flexibly.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} Clinicians shall be able to navigate between all implemented stages of the annotation workflow without losing saved work. The system shall indicate both the current and completed stages of the assessment.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_Label6}:] The system shall allow clinicians to assign a role to each speaker distinguished by the system, and to filter assessment transcripts by speaker role.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} As mentioned in \rref{FR_SpeakerIdentification}, the system distinguishes between speakers in automatically generated transcripts, but it makes educated guesses and does not know who each speaker is. Clinicians need to identify the child's speech and separate it from the adults, such as the parent or clinician, to analyze the child's language and identify interfering parent behavior. The system therefore separates the speakers, and the clinician assigns each one a role.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} The system must label every transcript segment from a distinguished speaker with their corresponding role once the clinician assigns it.
\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_Label7}:] The system shall generate detailed assessment reports from transcriptions, including any edits made by clinicians.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians review and correct the automatically generated transcription, adjust flagged assessment questions, and add notes to individual questions. The report must reflect these changes so that it includes the clinician’s judgment.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} The system must include any changes made throughout the analysis process made by the clinician, otherwise it outputs the automatically generated content in the report.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_Label8}:] The system shall allow clinicians to edit and update assessment results at any time.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians do not always analyze assessments sequentially. They may notice an error in a result or change their judgment after reviewing more of the recording, so they need to be able to go back and change results at any stage. 
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} The system must display the updated result once the clinician submits a change.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_Label9}:] The system shall display a list of all assigned patients to the logged-in clinician.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians care for multiple patients and need access to their information to properly care for each one. Additionally, they need a quick starting point for their work. 
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} The system must display every patient assigned to the clinician once they log in. The system must display an empty list when the clinician has no assigned patients.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_Label10}:] The system shall allow clinicians to add new patients.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians regularly take on new patients and need to create a profile for each one so that the patient’s assessments and records can be managed in the system.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} The system must add the new patient to the clinician’s list of patients once the clinician creates the profile.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_Label11}:] The system shall display to the clinician all completed assessments for a selected patient, ordered from most recent to oldest.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians must be able to track a patient's progress across multiple assessments over time.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} For a selected patient, all completed assessments are listed, ordered from most recent to oldest by assessment date.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_Label12}:] The system shall display an overview of every assessment's results, including automatically flagged items and the patient's overall results, without requiring a transcript.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} When reviewing many assessments, clinicians need a quick summary without having to perform any in-depth transcription and annotation.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} The overview for each completed assessment shows the patient's overall results, and each question's result (e.g., right or wrong) and whether it was flagged. The overview can be viewed before the assessment is reviewed in the SAFA tool.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_Label13}:] The system shall flag questions in video assessments where potential interference occurred.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} During assessment, outside help or disruptions (e.g., parents giving hints to the child) can heavily impact results and potentially make them invalid.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} Each question with detected interference is visibly marked in the assessment overview.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_Label14}:] The system shall allow the clinician to navigate to the point in the video assessment where a selected question was asked or a selected moment occurred.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} When searching for a specific moment or question asked in an assessment, when a transcript is available, clinicians should not have to search through a full recording to review that moment.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} Selecting a question or specific moment in a conversation starts video playback at that specific point in the assessment.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_Label15}:] The system shall allow the clinician to choose which questions or portions of an assessment to open in the SAFA tool for in-depth transcription and annotation.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians don't always need a full transcript of every assessment. After looking at the overview (\rref{FR_Label12}), they may only want to go deeper on a few flagged or unclear questions, which saves time compared to annotating the whole recording.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} From the assessment overview, the clinician can select one or more questions, or the full assessment, and the system opens only those portions of the recording and transcript in the SAFA tool.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_ManualEvaluation}:] The system shall allow clinicians to manually evaluate assessment questions that cannot be graded automatically, while playing back the portion of the recording in which the question was answered.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Some question types, such as repetition questions where the child repeats a sentence, have no single correct option that the system can compare against. The clinician must listen to and watch the child’s response to judge it, and the recording is the evidence for that judgment.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} For every question that is not automatically graded, the system must let the clinician play the recording segment for that question and record an evaluation (e.g., correct or incorrect). The saved evaluation must be shown with the question and included in the assessment’s score.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_QuestionNotes}:] The system shall allow clinicians to add, edit, and delete a note on each individual assessment question.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians record observations about specific answers, such as the type of error made or potential speech clues for that question's response. These observations support the final evaluation and must stay attached to the question they describe.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} After the clinician saves a note on a question, the system must show that note with the question every time the assessment is reopened, unless the clinician edits or deletes it.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_AssessmentNotes}:] The system shall allow clinicians to add, edit, and delete notes for an entire video assessment.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Some observations apply to the whole session rather than a single question, such as the child’s attention, the home environment, or the recording quality. Clinicians need a place to record these alongside the assessment.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} After the clinician saves a note on an assessment, the system must show that note on the assessment’s view every time the assessment is reopened, unless the clinician edits or deletes it.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_AnnotationsInRecord}:] The system shall store completed transcript annotations in the patient’s assessment record.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} SAFA currently runs as a standalone tool, and its annotations exist only in files that clinicians export and move by hand. Annotations need to stay linked to the assessment and patient they belong to, so they can be reviewed later and used in reports.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} After a clinician saves an annotation, the annotation must appear in that patient’s assessment record when it is opened from any of the clinician’s views, with no file upload or download step performed by the clinician.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_TranscriptHighlight}:]The system shall highlight the transcript segment that corresponds to the current playback position of the assessment recording.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians verify transcripts by watching and listening to the recording. Highlighting the segment being spoken lets them follow along without searching for their place in the transcript.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} While the recording plays, the highlighted segment must be the segment whose start and end times contain the current playback position, and the transcript must scroll so that the highlighted segment is visible.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_SegmentSeek}:] The system shall move the recording’s playback position to the start of a transcript segment when the clinician selects that segment.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians often need to re-watch a specific moment, such as a child’s answer or a flagged interaction, to verify the transcript or their evaluation. Jumping directly to it avoids scrubbing through the recording by hand.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} After the clinician selects a segment, the playback position must be set to the segment’s start time, and that segment must be highlighted.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_Label22}:] The system shall display assessment results in a format suited to the assessment’s purpose.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians use progress-monitoring assessments to track change over time and diagnostic or screening assessments to evaluate a patient at a single point in time. Each purpose requires a different view of the results to be interpreted correctly.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} The system must show progress-monitoring results as change over time and diagnostic or screening results as a single-session summary.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_Label23}:] The system shall organize assessment results by clinically meaningful domains, such as semantics, grammar, and phonology.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians interpret a child's language abilities by domain, so grouping results this way supports diagnosis and reporting.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} Every question in an assessment is assigned to a domain, and the clinician can view results grouped and summarized by domain.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_Label24}:] The system shall provide breaks during assessments to keep children engaged.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Children with limited attention or complex communication needs may disengage during long screen-based assessments.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} The system presents an interactive break activity (e.g., an animation) after a set number of questions defined for each assessment, after which the assessment resumes where it left off.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_ResultVisualization}:] The system shall visualize a patient’s assessment results by clinical domain (areas of speech) and language.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians who tested the previous platform valued charts and percentages, and asked for results by language to be shown side by side and grouped into domains such as semantics, grammar, and phonology, to help tell a language difference from a disorder. This requirement is outside the current project scope and is linked to the Support Multiple Assessment Comparison stretch goal from the Problem Statement.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} For an assessment completed in more than one language, the system must display the results for each clinical domain in a single, consolidated view.

\refstepcounter{reqnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthereqnum} \label{FR_LinguisticPatternDetection}:] The system shall automatically identify potential grammatical and phonological errors in the assessment transcript and present them to the clinician as suggestions for review.
\\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians see transcription as an intermediate step, and they asked for the system to identify grammatical and error patterns so they can focus on interpretation. Since ambiguous speech (e.g., a dialect feature versus an articulation error) can be clinically meaningful, detections must remain suggestions that the clinician confirms. This requirement is outside the current project scope.
\\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} Each detected error must be linked to its transcript segment and shown as unverified until the clinician accepts or rejects it. Only accepted errors may appear in the assessment report.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Relationships} \label{sec:Relationships}
The relationships between the data entities are as follows:

\begin{itemize}
\item Each clinician, researcher, and administrator has one user account, and each audit log entry refers to the user account that performed the action.
\item A clinician is assigned zero or more patients (one to many), and each patient is assigned to exactly one clinician.
\item Each patient has zero or more assessments.
\item An assessment has one recording, at most one transcript, one question response for each question asked, zero or more assessment notes, and zero or more reports.
\item A transcript is made up of an ordered list of transcript segments.
\item An interference flag belongs to the question response during which it occurred.
\item Question responses and transcript segments are linked by time: the segments within a question’s period of the recording form the transcript for that question. A question’s period starts when the previous question was answered (or at the start of the recording, for the first question) and ends when the patient answers it.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Annotations} \label{sec:Annotations}

In this document, an \textbf{annotation} is any information that is added to an assessment recording to describe it, beyond the recording itself. Annotations are generated automatically or by the clinician, and are then reviewed and corrected by the clinician. They are stored across the entities above:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Transcript annotations} (Transcript Segment): the text of each segment, its speaker and language, and its start and end times.
\item \textbf{Question annotations} (Question Response): the clinician’s evaluation of the answer and the note on the question.
\item \textbf{Behavior annotations} (Interference Flag): moments of possible interference detected by the system or added by the clinician, and whether the clinician kept each one.
\item \textbf{Session annotations} (Assessment Note): the clinician’s notes on the assessment as a whole.
\end{itemize}

Every annotation also carries metadata describing where it came from, so that clinicians can judge how much to rely on it (\nfrref{NFR-UN1}) and so that changes can be traced (\nfrref{NFR-S4}):

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Source:} whether it was generated automatically or added by a clinician.
\item \textbf{Verification:} whether a clinician has reviewed it.
\item \textbf{Original version:} for automatically generated annotations that a clinician has edited, the unedited version.
\item \textbf{History:} who created or changed it and when, recorded in the audit log.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Privacy and Data Retention} \label{sec:PrivacyRetention}

The system stores recordings, transcripts, and health information about children, so the following apply to all data listed above:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Access control:} A clinician can only access the patients, assessments, recordings, transcripts, and reports of patients assigned to them (\nfrref{NFR-S1}). Guardians can only access their own child’s assessments on the patient-facing site.
  \item \textbf{Encryption:} Recordings, transcripts, and patient records are encrypted in transit and in storage (\nfrref{NFR-S3}). 
  \item \textbf{Consent:} A guardian must consent to the recording of assessments and their processing by third-party services before the patient can complete an assessment.
  \item \textbf{Third-party processing:} Audio is sent to external speech recognition and keyword detection services (Whisper and Deepgram). Voice recordings can identify a patient, so these services must be covered by a Business Associate Agreement. Only the audio needed for transcription is sent, without names or other patient details.
  \item \textbf{Exports and reports:} Downloaded reports, transcripts, exported data, and video clips leave the system’s protection. Only the assigned clinician may export them, each export is recorded, and exported reports are marked as confidential.
  \item \textbf{Development data:} Real patient data is never to be used in development or testing.
  \item \textbf{Retention:} Patient records, recordings, transcripts, and reports are kept for the RETENTION period, after which they are deleted. Backups follow the same retention period (\nfrref{NFR-M1}).
  \item \textbf{Deletion:} When a guardian withdraws consent or requests deletion, the patient’s recordings, transcripts, and assessment data are deleted, including from backups, within DELETE\_WINDOW, except where a regulation requires them to be kept.
  \item \textbf{Auditing:} Every view, change, download, and export of patient information is recorded with the user and time, and these records are kept for LOG\_RETENTION (\nfrref{NFR-S4}).
  \item \textbf{Compliance:} The system is designed to meet the HIPAA technical safeguards (\nfrref{NFR-CO1}). Administrative and physical safeguards, such as staff training and breach notification procedures, are the responsibility of the institutions that use the system and are outside the scope of this project.
\end{itemize}

\section{Non-Functional Requirements} \label{sec:NFR}

\subsection{Usability}

\noindent \begin{itemize}

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-U1}
  The system shall be intuitive for users with limited technical fluency.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians’ technical fluency varies, and they have little time to learn new software. An intuitive system shortens the learning curve and avoids the need for formal training.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} In usability testing, at least TASK\_SUCCESS\_RATE\% of clinicians with no prior training on the system must complete each core task (e.g., assign a speaker role, view assessment results, add a patient) without assistance within TASK\_TIME minutes.

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-U2}
  The system shall present all functionality with clear and descriptive labels.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians work under heavy workloads, and they may be unfamiliar with common software icons. Clear labeling lets them recognize what each function does without guessing.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} In usability testing, at least TASK\_SUCCESS\_RATE\% of clinicians must be able to locate and use each core function on their first attempt, relying only on the labels shown on screen and without assistance.

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-U3}
  The system shall allow clinicians to return to any previously visited stage of their workflow with minimal effort.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians do not analyze assessments in a straight line. They may go back to review or correct earlier work, so moving between stages should not slow them down.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} The clinician must be able to reach any previously visited stage of the workflow from the current page in at most MAX\_NAV\_STEPS navigation steps.

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-U4}
  The system shall immediately show clinicians the result of each change they submit.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians edit sensitive patient information; therefore, they need quick confirmation that the system received their change and that it was applied correctly. Without immediate feedback, they cannot tell whether their action was registered, which causes uncertainty and repeated attempts.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} The system must display the updated information to the clinician within SYSTEM\_FEEDBACK\_TIME seconds after the clinician submits a change.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Security}

\noindent \begin{itemize}

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-S1}
  The system shall only allow a clinician to view the information of patients assigned to them.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Patient assessments are sensitive health information. Access must be limited to the clinician responsible for the patient in order to protect patient privacy and meet data protection obligations.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} The system must only list patients assigned to the logged-in clinician and must not allow a clinician to add or access a patient who is in another clinician’s care.

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-S2}
  The system shall require users to re-enter their credentials after IDLE\_TIMEOUT minutes of inactivity.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians may leave their workstation unattended, and their accounts contain highly sensitive information. Automatic sign-out prevents unauthorized people from viewing it.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} The system must end the user’s session and require credentials before displaying any patient information once the user has been inactive for IDLE\_TIMEOUT minutes.

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-S3}
  The system shall encrypt patient recordings and transcripts in transit and in storage.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Assessment recordings and transcripts contain private patient information. The annotation service sends video assessments to a third-party transcription service, and the recordings are also kept in the system’s storage. Hence, they must be protected in both places so that anyone who intercepts the data or gains access to the storage cannot read it.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} The system must transmit all recordings and transcripts to third-party services using TRANSIT\_ENCRYPTION\_STANDARD and must store them using STORAGE\_ENCRYPTION\_STANDARD.

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-S4}
  The system shall record who accessed or changed patient information, and when.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} HIPAA requires a record of activity in systems that contain patient health information, so that unauthorized access can be detected and investigated. Assessment results also inform clinical decisions and reports, so clinicians may need to review how a result changed over time.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} For every view, change, download, or export of a patient’s records, recordings, transcripts, evaluations, notes, annotations, or reports, the system must store the identity of the user, the action performed, the item affected, and the date and time. These records must not be editable by clinicians or researchers and must be kept for LOG\_RETENTION.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Maintainability}

\noindent \begin{itemize}

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-M1}
  The system shall regularly back up patient information and assessment recordings.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Assessment data is difficult or impossible to recreate, so losing it would waste clinicians’ work and could affect patient care.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} The system must back up all patient information and assessment recordings at least every BACKUP\_INTERVAL hours.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Recording Quality}

\noindent \begin{itemize}

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-P1}
  The system shall only accept video assessments in which the speakers can be clearly seen and heard.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} The annotation tool and behavior detection depend on the recording’s quality. Poor audio makes the auto-generated transcript less accurate, whereas poor video quality makes it difficult to detect faces and parent interference. Both instances can lead clinicians to wrong conclusions about a child’s assessment.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} The system must only accept submitted assessments where the recording at least meets the minimum video resolution, MIN\_VIDEO\_RESOLUTION, and the minimum audio level, MIN\_AUDIO\_LEVEL.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Accuracy}

\noindent \begin{itemize}

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-A1}
  The system shall raise automatic flags that are accurate enough for clinicians to use them to decide which parts of an assessment need review, with clinicians still making the final judgement.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} To make things easier, clinicians can use the flags in the assessment overview (\rref{FR_Label12}) or the interference markers (\rref{FR_Label13}) to quickly identify which segments need in-depth analysis. Too many missed flags mean certain signals go unnoticed by clinicians, while too many false flags waste clinicians' time.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} On a set of assessments reviewed by a clinician, the system flags at least FLAG\_DETECTION\_RATE\% of the moments the clinician identifies, and no more than FLAG\_FALSE\_ALARM\_RATE\% of its flags are false alarms. Each flag is placed within FLAG\_TIME\_TOLERANCE seconds of when the moment occurs in the recording.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Performance}

\noindent \begin{itemize}

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-PF1}
  The system shall respond quickly enough that clinicians can review assessments without noticeable waiting or lag.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians review many assessments in one sitting, so small delays can add up, especially if they are working on a tight schedule.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} On a connection of at least MIN\_BANDWIDTH, the assessment overview (\rref{FR_Label12}) loads within OVERVIEW\_LOAD\_TIME seconds. Jumping to a question or segment in the recording (\rref{FR_Label14}) is covered by \nfrref{NFR-P2}.

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-P2}
  The system shall keep the transcript synchronized with the recording during playback and navigation.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians verify transcripts and evaluate answers by matching text to what they see and hear. If the highlighted segment lags behind the recording, or a selected segment starts at the wrong moment, clinicians may attribute speech to the wrong segment or question.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} For recordings up to MAX\_REC\_LEN minutes long, the difference between the playback position and the stored start time of the highlighted or selected segment must not exceed SYNC\_TOL milliseconds, and playback must begin within SEEK\_TIME seconds of the clinician selecting a segment.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Understandability}

\noindent \begin{itemize}

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-UN1}
  The system shall make it clear to clinicians which content was generated automatically and which has been verified or edited by a clinician.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians emphasized that automated results are cues for review, not final judgments. To decide what still needs their attention and how much to rely on a result, they need to understand at a glance where each transcript segment, score, and flag came from.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} In usability testing, at least TASK\_SUCCESS\_RATE\% of clinicians must correctly identify which items on a screen are unverified automated content and which are clinician-verified, without assistance.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Compliance}

\noindent \begin{itemize}

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-CO1}
  The system shall meet the technical safeguards of the HIPAA Security Rule for the patient health information it stores and processes.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} The system stores recordings, transcripts, and assessment results of children, which are protected health information for U.S.-based clinicians. Removing names is not enough to de-identify this data: under HIPAA, full-face images and voice recordings can themselves identify a patient. Clinicians identified HIPAA compliance and control over who can access data as conditions for adopting the system, and institutions must approve tools before clinicians can use them \citep{CDC_HIPAA_1996}.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} Each HIPAA technical safeguard (access control, audit controls, integrity, person or entity authentication, and transmission security) must be mapped to at least one implemented mechanism in the system, and a review of the system against this mapping must find no unaddressed safeguard. Every third-party service that stores or processes patient health information, including recordings and audio, must be covered by a Business Associate Agreement.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Capacity}

\noindent \begin{itemize}

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-CA1}
  The system shall store the patients, assessments, recordings, and annotations of a full clinical caseload for every clinician using it.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians may each care for 30 or more patients, and every assessment adds a recording, a transcript, and annotations that must be kept for the full retention period. The system must hold this data without clinicians having to delete older assessments to make room for new ones.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} With NUM\_CLINICIANS clinicians, each with CASELOAD patients who have each completed ASMTS\_PER\_PT assessments with recordings of up to MAX\_REC\_LEN minutes, the system must store all of the data and still meet \nfrref{NFR-U4} and \nfrref{NFR-P2}.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Reliability}

\noindent \begin{itemize}

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-RE1}
The system shall reliably generate transcriptions for supported patient assessment videos.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Transcriptions are essential to the annotation workflow. Failed or incomplete transcription generation can delay assessments and prevent accurate identification of speakers (\rref{FR_SpeakerIdentification}).
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} For at least GENERATE\_SUCCESS\% of valid, supported assessment videos tested, the system shall successfully generate a transcription as specified in {\rref{FR_AutomaticTranscription}} without requiring the clinician to restart the process manually.

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-RE2}
The system shall preserve saved transcription edits and annotations across sessions.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians may complete assessments over multiple sessions. Losing saved work would increase workload, compromise record-keeping, and potentially affect the consistency of assessment results.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} For at least SAVE\_SUCCESS\% of saved transcription edits (\rref{FR_TranscriptionEditing}) and annotation data (\rref{FR_AnnotationExport}) tested, the data shall remain available and unchanged after the clinician ends the session, logs back in, and reopens the assessment.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Convenience}

\noindent \begin{itemize}

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-CV1}
The system shall respond to clinician interactions within an acceptable time throughout the assessment process.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians need to review and annotate assessments efficiently. Delayed responses can interrupt their workflow and increase the time required to complete assessments.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} At least AVG\_INTERACTIONS\% of common interactions related to transcription editing and annotation data export (\rref{FR_AnnotationExport}) shall complete within INTERACTION\_TIME seconds under normal operating conditions, excluding processing-intensive tasks.

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-CV2}
The system shall allow clinicians to access their annotation work from different devices through a web browser.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians may need to access assessment records from different work environments. Browser-based access reduces reliance on a specific device and eliminates the need to manually transfer annotation files.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} Clinicians shall be able to access and continue editing the same saved transcription edits and annotation data (\rref{FR_TranscriptionEditing}, \rref{FR_AnnotationExport}) from at least two supported devices through a web browser without manually transferring files.

\refstepcounter{nfrnum}
\item[\textbf{\rthenfrnum}:]
\label{NFR-CV3}
The system shall minimize repetitive interactions when clinicians complete annotations and manage patient assessments.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Repetitive actions, such as manually saving work or repeatedly entering the same information already known to the system, increase administrative workload and can interrupt the assessment process.
  \\ \textbf{Fit Criterion:} The system shall automatically preserve changes to transcription edits and annotation data when clinicians navigate between assessment workflow stages (\rref{FR_AssessmentWorkflowNavigation}), and shall pre-fill fields with information already stored in the system (e.g., patient name and assessment date) so that the clinician does not need to re-enter it.

\end{itemize}

\section{AI and Machine Learning Requirements} \label{sec:AIML}

The system integrates a speech recognition model that was suggested and provided by the capstone team’s supervisors. 
The model was not created by the capstone team. \\

The model used is OpenAI’s Whisper-1, a general-purpose, multilingual automatic speech recognition and translation model. 
It is accessed through an API and takes audio or text as input \citep{OpenAI_Whisper}.

\subsection{Model Inputs}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Individual segments of the assessment’s audio file (.wav), each under 25 MB.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Model Outputs} \label{sec:AIModelOutputs}

\begin{itemize}
    \item A verbose JSON object containing the following \citep{openai-create-transcription}:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \texttt{duration} (double): duration of the input audio
        \item \texttt{language} (string): language of the input audio
        \item \texttt{text} (string): the transcribed text
        \item \texttt{segments} (array of JSON objects, optional): details of the transcribed text, given in segments. Each segment contains the following:
        \begin{itemize}
            \item \texttt{id}: a unique identification number
            \item \texttt{avg\_logprob} (float): the average log probability of the segment
            \item \texttt{compression\_ratio} (float): the compression ratio of the segment
            \item \texttt{end} (double): the end time of the segment in seconds
            \item \texttt{no\_speech\_prob} (float): the probability that there is no speech in the segment
            \item \texttt{seek}: the seek offset
            \item \texttt{start} (double): the start time of the segment in seconds
            \item \texttt{temperature} (float): the temperature parameter used for generating the segment
            \item \texttt{text} (string): the content of the segment
            \item \texttt{tokens} (array of numbers): the token IDs for the text content of the segment
        \end{itemize}
        \item \texttt{usage} (object, optional): usage statistics for the model, given the input audio duration
        \begin{itemize}
            \item \texttt{seconds} (double): the duration of the input audio in seconds
            \item \texttt{type}: the type of the usage object, which is always \texttt{duration}
        \end{itemize}
        \item \texttt{words} (array, optional): the words and their corresponding timestamps
        \begin{itemize}
            \item \texttt{end} (double): the end time of the word in seconds
            \item \texttt{start} (double): the start time of the word in seconds
            \item \texttt{word} (string): the text content of the word
        \end{itemize}
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Example Output} \label{sec:ExampleOutput}
\begin{verbatim}

  "language": "english",
  "text": "I like cats and dogs",

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
The model is not currently being evaluated. The annotation tool does, however, let speech clinicians edit transcriptions in chunks, viewing the text next to the audio playback, so incorrect auto-generated transcriptions can be corrected.

As mentioned in {\hyperref[sec:AIModelOutputs]{Section~\ref*{sec:AIModelOutputs}: AI Model Outputs}}, the model's verbose JSON output also includes values for each transcript segment that indicate how reliable the transcription is: \texttt{avg\_logprob} (the average log probability of the segment), \texttt{no\_speech\_prob} (the probability that the segment contains no speech), and \texttt{compression\_ratio} (high values can indicate repetitive and/or hallucinated text). Converting \texttt{avg\_logprob} to a probability gives a rough confidence score for each segment. The system could display this score next to each transcript segment and highlight segments that fall below a threshold, so clinicians can review the least certain parts of a transcript first and decide where to spend their time.

It should be noted however, these values are the model's own estimates, and the model can be wrong. Hence, the confidence scores should only be a guide to a clinician's review.

The model could be evaluated in the future by comparing the original auto-generated transcriptions against the clinician-edited versions and calculating the word error rate. 
\subsection{Known Limitations} \label{sec:KnownLimitations}

The model’s website describes its performance as average, so the following failure modes are expected when it is used in TeLaC \citep{Laumann_Whisper_2022}.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Incorrect transcription or translation:} since the model’s overall performance is average, some transcribed or translated text can be wrong. If these errors go unnoticed, clinicians may draw incorrect conclusions about a child’s speech abilities.
  \item \textbf{Incorrect transcription of difficult non-English words: }: the model is known to incorrectly transcribe difficult words in languages other than English. This is concerning for TeLaC considering the system is used to assess bilingual children.
  \item \textbf{Uneven performance across different languages:} The model generally performs best on languages with a decent amount of training data, such as Spanish, German, Italian, and English. Transcript accuracy may vary by language, and languages with fewer resources may be transcribed less reliably.
\end{itemize}

Because of these expected failures, TeLaC doesn’t treat the model’s output as final. 
Clinicians review the auto-generated transcript against the video playback and correct any errors before relying on it.

\section{Likely Changes}    
The following changes are expected during or after the project. Parts of the
system affected by these changes should be designed so that they can be modified
without redesigning the rest of the system.

\section{Likely Changes}    

\noindent \begin{itemize}

\item[\textbf{LC\refstepcounter{lcnum}\thelcnum}\label{LC_AssessmentFunctionality}:] Assessment functionality: The types of annotations and transcription editing capabilities may change as clinicians identify more important functionality.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} SAFA is still under development, and the research team's study reports several clinician feature requests that have not yet been prioritized. The interface redesign and usability testing are also likely to reveal new needs, so the annotation and editing features should be easy to extend.

\item[\textbf{LC\refstepcounter{lcnum}\thelcnum}\label{LC_DataVisualization}:] Data visualization: The types of visualizations available to clinicians and researchers may change as requirements for tracking patient progress develop and patient data increases.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Clinicians asked for charts, percentages, and side-by-side comparisons of results by language and domain, but the exact views have not been specified. As more assessments are collected for each patient, new ways of summarizing progress over time will also become useful.

\item[\textbf{LC\refstepcounter{lcnum}\thelcnum}\label{LC_ReportGeneration}:] Report generation: The content and formatting of assessment reports may evolve as clinicians relay professional standards and needs.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Report requirements are currently based on a small number of clinician comments, and the clinics, schools, and hospitals that employ SLPs may each require specific report contents or formats.

\item[\textbf{LC\refstepcounter{lcnum}\thelcnum}\label{LC_ReportTypes}:] Report types: Assessment reports may change to accommodate various audiences, from researchers to patients. This would affect the language, confidentiality of information, and formatting of reports.
  \\ \textbf{Rationale:} Patient-facing reports are a stretch goal of the project. A report written for guardians would need plain language and must exclude clinical notes that are not meant for them, while a report for researchers may need de-identified data.

\end{itemize}


\section{Unlikely Changes}
The following are not expected to change. The system is designed on the
assumption that they hold.
\noindent
\begin{itemize}

\subsection{Appendix B: Development Plan} \label{sec:AppendixB}

Development of the system will be split into three phases, with each phase 
building on the previous one so that the most important goals are completed first.
Newer features are then added once the main system is complete and functional. 
The timing of each phase then follows the milestones outlined in the Development Plan.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Phase 1: Integration.} The first phase focuses on integrating SAFA
  into the existing TeLaC platform, so clinicians can review and annotate assessments
  without ever having to leave the platform. This includes connecting SAFA's existing transcription
  and annotation Python modules to TeLaC's patient records and recorded assessments,
  and making sure that patient data stays secure as it moves in between. This phase
  covers the \textit{Integrate SAFA Into the TeLaC platform} and \textit{Protect
  Patient Data} goals, and will be demonstrated in the proof of concept.

  \item \textbf{Phase 2: Enhance the SAFA User Interface.} Once SAFA is
  integrated, the second phase focuses on improving its interface so that
  speech clinicians can use it with ease, regardless of their knowledge and
  technical expertise. This involves making the annotation workflow simpler and more
  intuitive, and requires addressing feedback from clinicians on the current SAFA tool (see
  Appendix A). This phase covers the \textit{Improve SAFA's Usability} and
  \textit{Speed Up Speech Annotation} goals.

  \item \textbf{Phase 3: New Features.} The team will add new features that extend the system beyond
  SAFA's current capabilities, such as the stretch goals from the Problem
  Statement. This includes generating patient-facing assessment reports and comparing multiple
  assessments for the same patient over time. Any features not completed during
  the course will be documented as future work.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection*{Question 2}
Creating user personas was my biggest challenge. 
The part of the system our group is working on is mainly for clinicians, so it was hard to identify different types of users, and we were worried the personas would end up looking nearly identical. 
Hence, we created different “types” of clinicians who might use the platform and differentiated them by their work environment and capabilities, such as how heavy their workload is and how comfortable they are with technology. 
This gave us personas that reflect real differences in how the system might be used, which also helped us write non-functional requirements around usability.

Additionally, deciding how detailed each use case should be was also a bit difficult. 
I wasn’t sure where in the workflow each one should start, and simulating the whole workflow from when the clinician enters the system felt like too much. 
It would have repeated the same steps, such as logging in and selecting a patient in every use case. 
Hence, I scoped each use case to a single clinician goal and used the preconditions to set the starting point. 
For example, the clinician is already logged in and has opened a patient’s assessment. 
I also kept the steps focused on what the clinician does and how the system responds, and left out screen-level details, since the interface may change during the redesign.
